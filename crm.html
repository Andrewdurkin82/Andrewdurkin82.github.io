Portfolio Study 05 · CRM + Case Management

Three CRM ideas. Two industries. The same underlying problem.

Speira, Nostos, and the Pizza Hut Customer Care CRM were never full production deployments. What they do show is a repeatable way of thinking about information systems: context should be easy to find, ownership should be visible, follow-up should be deliberate, and history should survive the person who happened to answer the phone.

SpeiraStudent service continuity and staff workflow.
NostosReturning-student recruitment tracking and handoffs.
Customer Care CRMIssue history, recovery, follow-up, and repeat patterns.

The recurring need

In both higher education and restaurant operations, the immediate problem looked different. Underneath, the failure mode was similar: important context lived in inboxes, handwritten notes, individual memory, disconnected records, or systems that were not designed around the frontline workflow.

FindGet to the right person or record quickly.
UnderstandSee the relevant history without reconstructing it from scratch.
OwnMake responsibility and next steps visible.
Follow throughKeep cases from disappearing after the first interaction.

Speira

Working prototype · Davenport

Designed as a Welcome Desk CRM and institutional service-history layer. Banner remains the system of record; Speira focused on the context staff need when a student appears, calls, or comes back with a problem that crossed departments.

Search-first designQuick View and Full Profile screens across roughly 1,000 synthetic student records.
ContinuityInteractions, cases, sticky notes, follow-ups, and “My Work” visibility.
ExplorationLightweight analytics and a natural-language “Who’s that one student?” search concept grounded in structured data.

Nostos

Operational workflow prototype · Davenport

Built around returning-student recruitment and re-entry follow-up. The problem was less “CRM” in the commercial sense and more case visibility: who owns the lead, what is blocking progress, and where does the student go next?

VolumeRoughly 1,000 submissions, about 800 unique students, with duplicate handling required.
Status modelNew, AR, Advising, re-entry appointments, waiting on information, declined, enrolled, and related handoff states.
Core valueOwnership, deduplication, handoffs, follow-up, and measurable enrollment progress instead of records disappearing into inboxes.

Pizza Hut Customer Care CRM

Standalone prototype · Pizza Hut

Started as a way to track troublesome customers and quickly became a broader customer-recovery system. The handwritten approach made it hard to know whether a customer had a pattern, what had already been promised, or whether someone still needed to follow up.

Customer recordLookup by contact/order details, issue history, notes, service-watch flags, and follow-up instructions.
Recovery trackingRefunds, credits, replacements, discounts, rewards, and related loss/waste visibility.
Pattern visibilityRepeat issues, high-attention customers, fake-order flags, and analytics around contact and loss types.

All three remained prototypes or workflow tools rather than fully governed production CRMs. Shared authentication, multi-user persistence, permissions, audit controls, and enterprise deployment would be required before treating the Pizza Hut prototype or Speira as production systems.

What the set demonstrates

The value is not that I designed three different databases. It is that the same architecture kept emerging from three different operational settings: searchable context, visible ownership, explicit status, durable history, and follow-up that survives shift changes and departmental handoffs.

That is the part I would carry into a real CRM, case-management, or ERP modernization project. The software matters, but first you have to understand what people are trying not to lose.

Different users. Different data. Same systems problem.

CRM work is ultimately continuity work: preserve the right context, make the next action obvious, and prevent the organization from depending on one person remembering everything.
